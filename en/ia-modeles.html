02 · The three layers

A model, an AI: what are we talking about?

People often confuse “an AI” with “a model”. The whole landscape fits into three stacked layers: the engine, what you see, and where your conversations go.

01

The model

It is the engine, the part that “thinks”. A giant neural network trained on massive corpora. In practice, a file of weights from a few gigabytes to several hundred. Open, you can download it and run it yourself; closed, only its publisher can run it.

02

The interface

It is what you see: the chat, the search bar, the voice assistant. Some companies do everything (OpenAI, Anthropic, Mistral, DeepSeek). Others only assemble an interface plugged into other people's models (Perplexity, Venice, Poe).

03

The data

It is where your conversations go. The interface does not decide this: whoever runs the model does. The publisher, a third-party interface, a GPU network, or your own computer.

04 · Open or closed

Four philosophies

Few companies publish everything or nothing. The real question is which part of the house stays behind the door.

Everything publishedNothing published
1

Open by principle

Even the flagship model is published. Its weights directly power other interfaces.

  • DeepSeek
  • Moonshot
  • Z.ai
2

Open core with tiers

Open core (Apache 2.0), commercial licence above a revenue threshold.

  • Mistral AI
3

Closed at the top, open downstream

The frontier stays closed while distribution models are opened: flood the ecosystem without giving away the flagship.

  • Google
  • Meta
  • Alibaba
  • xAI
4

Closed

No downloadable weights today: the models only run on the publisher's servers.

  • OpenAI
  • Anthropic

A model's status is a dated fact: Mistral opened its Medium, Alibaba closed its Max, xAI re-closed after Grok-1.

05 · Your data

Where do your conversations go?

Four possible routes. The look of the interface says nothing about the destination: you have to check who runs the model.

1

Closed model, official interface

YouInterfacePublisher's servers

Your conversations go to the publisher, tied to your account. GPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok: US clouds. The DeepSeek, Kimi and Qwen apps: servers in China, even though the weights are open.

2

Third-party interface

YouInterfaceModel publisher

The interface's servers first, then the publisher of the chosen model. Perplexity applies standard retention. Venice keeps nothing, but if you pick a closed model, its publisher still sees the request.

3

Self-hosted open model

YouYour servers

The weights are downloaded and run inside your company or administration. Everything stays with you: this is the path of national and sovereign models.

4

Local

YouYour computer

With Ollama or Opera, the model runs on your machine. Nothing leaves the computer, even offline.

Perplexity

An augmented search engine. Its Sonar model, a Llama derivative, lives alongside GPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok and Kimi: six arrows come into it in the diagram.

Venice

No in-house model: Venice runs open models on decentralized GPUs, with zero retention. Nothing is stored on its servers.

The rule to remember

Your data stays where the model runs. Choosing an AI means choosing who runs the model.
